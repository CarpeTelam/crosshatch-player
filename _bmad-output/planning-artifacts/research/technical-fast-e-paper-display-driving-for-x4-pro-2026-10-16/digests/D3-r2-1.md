## Answer to the gating question
Yes on structure, unknown on the floor. The SSD1677 defines per-pixel LUT selection by the (RED-RAM bit, BW-RAM bit) pair into LUT0..LUT3, with LUT4 as VCOM, and command 0x32 writes all five LUTs' VS rows independently plus 10 groups x (TP A-D, RP) and 5 frame-rate bytes; voltages go via 0x03/0x04/0x2C. Waveshare's own 4-gray mode is exactly "two planes as a 2-bit selector into four VS rows loaded through 0x32", triggered with 0x22 = 0xC7 (no OTP load), so a volatile MCU LUT is honoured as long as 0x22 bit 4 stays clear. The differences from the SSD1681 are mechanical (10 groups vs 12, 5-byte timing rows, 105 bytes to 0x32 instead of 153, no 0x3F, no 0x3A/0x3B). A one-frame LUT lies inside the documented ranges; no minimum is stated. Not evidenced: the frame period (no Hz table, frame-rate byte encoding undocumented, gate-line-width commands reserved), so the shortest one-frame update at 800x480 is unknown; the only family figure is SSD1675's 15-200 Hz range (5-67 ms per frame), which must not be assumed for the 1677. The 0x22 value matching InkBoy's needs (analog kept on, no OTP load, mode 2) is 0xCC; 0x0C is the same without re-asserting clock/analog.

## Leads
- Measure the SSD1677 frame period empirically: load a LUT with one group TP[0A]=N for N=1,10,100 at frame-rate bytes 0x22 vs other values and time BUSY.
- A newer datasheet revision or Solomon app note may document the frame-rate byte table.
- 0x37 "RAM ping-pong" for display mode 2 may be a hardware old/new swap that avoids re-uploading both planes.
- Conflict noted by the researcher: an Xteink X3 gist says the X4 uses an SSD1680; papyrix and the freeink-sdk treat the X4/X4 Pro original batch as SSD1677 (the SDK's driver and the OEM-verified LUT settle this for the lead).
