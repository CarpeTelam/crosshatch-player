# D4 round 1 digest: implementation reality, limits, and risk on ESP32-S3

Researcher budget: 18 tool calls (6 searches, 7 pages read, 6 snippets). Q1 (SPI floor) found no measured e-paper numbers; arithmetic only.

## Key excerpts

idfxx SSD1680 readme (fast mode):
> "temperature-register forcing selecting a shortened OTP waveform"
> "Quality varies by panel batch and temperature — validate on hardware, and prefer `full` refreshes when in doubt."
> "Fast refresh trades contrast for durability of the image." [black pixels] "look black at first, but the next partial refresh settles them to a visibly gray level"
> "issue a `full` refresh every ~10 partial refreshes to clean accumulated ghosting."

Frontiers in Physics 2021 (Wang et al.):
> "the rules of direct current (DC) balance are also followed to prevent the charge from getting trapped in the driving backplane."
> "the DC balance is not considered in the design of the driving waveform, which may cause damage to the EPD"
> "a unit of time is 1/50 = 20 ms if the frame rate is 50 frames/s" ... "the driving time of the new driving waveform is reduced by nearly 50%."

Hackaday 2026-07-21 / Tony Weston:
> "was recently able to get 20 FPS out of an ESP32-based device without using any extra processing power" (960x540, parallel-bus panel, 4 greys)
> calibration: "lots of trial and error"; "the movie shows blacks as greys"
> commenter: "a lot of ghosting and grayscale remnants"
> commenter (unverified): ~10M refreshes per pixel => ~139 hours at 20 FPS

Pimoroni Inky Frame 7.3 thread (2024):
> refreshed every ten minutes for months, "a few tens of thousands of refreshes"; problem "grew over months"; after double refreshes "looking a lot, lot better" ... "this also seems to mean it isn't permanent damage"; on USB power burn-in rebuilt "within about a dozen refreshes".

OpenELAB GDEQ0426T82 (SSD1677, 800x480): full 3.5 s, fast 1.5 s, partial 0.42 s.

## Leads

- Tony Weston's EPD_Painter page is 540 KB; only the first 100 KB was read. The remainder likely holds frames-per-update, per-frame drive time, and the scanner-based verification method. Fetch with offset 100000+.
- The "Game Boy at 30 fps on ESP32 e-paper" item (zeli.app aggregator of a Hacker News post) was not traced to a primary source; commenters there raised per-panel grayscale calibration and temperature drift.
- idfxx SSD1680 driver repo (github.com/cleishm/idfxx) contains the actual temperature-forcing values and the Good Display gray4 LUT; best SPI-controller reference found; its readme is the only source that explicitly names panel-batch variance.
- Contradiction: SSD1677 "fast" timing is 1.5 s (OpenELAB, Good Display touch variants) vs 0.76 s (another reseller). Needs the Good Display datasheet.
- Contradiction on durability: user reports (Pimoroni) show residue is recoverable; patents and the Frontiers paper say DC imbalance causes backplane charge trapping. The recoverable case was a vendor OTP waveform, not a custom one, so it does not test the custom-LUT risk.
- epdiy (vroland) was never reached by search; its waveform tooling for parallel panels is the main candidate for open calibration tooling.

## Looked for, could not find

- Any measured SPI transfer time for a 48 KB e-paper frame on ESP32-S3 at 20/40 MHz with vs without DMA, or any measurement of whether 0x44/0x45 window writes help on SSD16xx.
- Any SSD16xx/UC81xx project reporting sustained updates-per-second with a custom shortened LUT and the frame-rate register values used; the 20 fps result is parallel-bus only.
- Any E Ink or Solomon Systech statement giving a refresh-count lifetime or quantified damage from short/DC-unbalanced waveforms (only "may cause damage" phrasing).
- Any open-source calibration rig (camera/photodiode) for custom LUTs; only "trial and error" plus flatbed-scanner verification was found.
- Any shipped open-source e-reader firmware with a 6-12 month retrospective on a custom fast mode (Xteink X4 search surfaced only release notes mentioning a "differential refresh" SDK method, with no user-complaint data).
- Vendor (Good Display) app note on partial-refresh limits; only third-party guidance (Inkplate 5-10/40-50 threshold, Adafruit 3-minute rule).
