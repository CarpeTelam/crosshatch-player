## Cross-dimension insights

- **The fork is closer to InkBoy than InkBoy was to its own starting point.** InkBoy began from Waveshare's stock driver, whose LUT path serves only the vendor tables [1][35]; the fork's SSD1677 driver already has custom-LUT upload, 0xCC activation, asynchronous refresh, windowed writes and a shadow plane [9]. The work is the scheduler, the counters, and the tuning, not the controller plumbing.
- **The same selector exists on all three X4 Pro batches, in two dialects** (D3). A per-pixel budget layer can sit above both, emitting hold or drive per pixel into whichever plane encoding the batch uses, while the waveform tables stay per batch and per panel.
- **The temperature trick is the lever the fork already pulls** (D4); the next step is the loaded LUT.
- **Scale, not the controller, is the difference from InkBoy.** Ten times the pixels means hundreds of kilobytes of per-pixel state (D2a, D4) that must live in PSRAM behind an S3-only capability guard and does not exist on the C3.
- **The Sticky is the second test bench, not a second target** (D5). Same controller, same LUT structure, same selector, different panel response, so it validates the design and defeats any shared tuning.
- **YACP's reinforcement and InkBoy's budgets attack the same graying problem from opposite ends** (D2a, D6). InkBoy stops driving settled pixels in software; YACP's waveform gives them a weak nudge in hardware. The fork's UltraChip drivers already hold the second, so the game loop's "hold" row can be tried both ways on those batches, while the SSD1677 batch gets only the first until someone builds the LUT.
- **The one number that decides the ceiling is the one nobody has.** With the frame period unknown, every fps projection for the X4 Pro is arithmetic on SPI time alone.

## Contrary evidence

The red-team pass did not run (`red_team` is off and validation is `normal`). The strongest counter-arguments came from the research itself: the Glider author's own table rates controller panels at "100ms to several seconds" total latency because of the host-to-controller data path [12]; the idfxx author documents that shortened waveforms under-drive blacks and vary by batch and temperature [18]; and E Ink's patents say DC-balanced drive is two to three times slower than imbalanced drive precisely because imbalance degrades electrodes [16]. None of these says the X4 Pro cannot reach 10 to 20 window scans per second; all of them say the result will be grayer, panel-specific, and of unknown long-term cost.

## Recommendations

Ordered for the stated priority: stable grayscale and no-flash first, speed as a bonus. Each item names what it feeds and the confidence its evidence carries.

1. **Run a grayscale calibration spike before planning the epic.** Feeds roadmap risk and estimates. On an SSD1677 X4 Pro, load a one-group one-frame LUT (TP[0A] = 1, the rest 0, Waveshare's frame-rate bytes 0x22 and two other byte values), activate with 0xCC, and time BUSY to get the frame period and the dose length; then port InkBoy's transition-test screen and find a first 12-entry dose table by eye, photographing each pass [1][31]. Time a full two-plane upload at 20 and 40 MHz as a side measurement, since the driver runs 40 MHz against a 20 MHz datasheet rating [9][31]. Two or three days. Confidence: datasheet structure verified [31]; dose behavior on this panel unknown.
2. **Carry the safety patterns from day one.** Feeds PRD constraints. Volatile LUT only with a build check that forbids the OTP write command, stock voltage tail, a cleaning full refresh on a count and on exit, a per-session cap, and an off-by-default setting with a lifespan warning, because damage is plausible but unquantified [1][15][16][17]. Confidence: risk verified by two publishers; magnitude unknown.
3. **Build InkBoy's grayscale state machine on SSD1677 as an S3-only opt-in capability, grayscale from day one.** Feeds the architecture spine and the game system's display contract. The model is a per-pixel state machine (level from 0 to 192, remaining doses, target, source) over the two-plane selector, with a 12-entry dose table and hold mask, interpolated retargets, optional history routes and a weak mode, driven by a slot scheduler that writes black and white masks to the BW and RED planes and ends each burst with an all-hold scan [1] (D2a checklist). Run it at whatever scan rate the panel and bus allow; a board game is served by a few scans per second, so prefer whole-screen correctness over windowed speed. Budget PSRAM for roughly 770 KB of pixel state and 1.15 MB of optional history at 800x480 (derived, D2a). Keep voltages at the panel's values and never touch the OTP command. Confidence: method verified from code, re-read twice [1]; SSD1677 mapping verified [31][32]; the author describes "a lot, and I mean a lot of calibration" on one panel, and it still leaves trails [1][5].
4. **Keep 1-bit dithered output as the fallback, not the first release.** Feeds the brief's feasibility. InkBoy's dithered mode is the same machinery with a 4-bit counter per pixel and no shade model [1]; it is the cheap path if the dose table proves unstable across batches, and the mode to ship for the UltraChip batches until their tables exist. Confidence: method verified from code.
5. **Port InkBoy's calibration tooling, not its numbers.** Feeds the architecture spine and test strategy. A transition-test screen, a live one-value-at-a-time tuning menu, JSON presets on the SD card, an FPS counter, exported defaults with a drift test, and a frozen reference planner with a differential host test, so each batch and tester can tune without a rebuild [1]. Numbers from a 200x200 SSD1681 do not transfer [1][13]. Confidence: tooling verified from code.
6. **Do not chase Ink-boy's driver; keep two of its ideas.** Feeds the architecture spine. Its only public artifacts are wrapper signatures and a self-reported 276 ms on one UC8279 unit [7]. Take the changed-rectangle send and drive with unchanged-frame skip and the per-phase microsecond timing log as instrumentation; if the author answers an issue, the DU frame-scaling code could save UC8279 work; otherwise the fork's own UC8179 register-LUT path is the better base [9][11]. Confidence: Ink-boy evidence low (single self-reported unit).
7. **Keep the UC8179 and UC8279 batches as a second phase.** Feeds roadmap. Use the old/new register-LUT dialect with WW and KK held at zero [11][27] and expect separate tuning per batch. Confidence: register layout verified from GxEPD2 and the SDK.
8. **Use the Sticky as the second bench, with its own tables.** Feeds roadmap and test strategy. Same controller and resolution, different panel response and sequences [9][39]; a shared pipeline with per-board waveform tables is the right shape, and shared dose numbers are not. Confidence: controller identity verified by two sources; panel behavior from the SDK's own notes.
9. **Use the existing non-flashing transition for the non-game UI on the UltraChip batches now, and as the game loop's hold candidate.** Feeds the architecture spine and the spike. This is the no-flash half of the priority with no new waveform work. The SDK's UC8179 and UC8279 drivers already carry the stock previous-to-current transition reachable through the grayscale-base call; YACP shows it can replace the flashing maintenance refresh on the X3 [9][43]. Measure its duration and ghosting on an X4 Pro UltraChip unit during the spike, and copy YACP's three rules (strong scrub after grayscale, settle once after a full, settle before sleep). Confidence: mechanism verified from both code bases; effect on the X4 Pro panel unmeasured, and YACP shipped it without hardware validation [44].

## Open questions

- **SSD1677 frame period at 800x480** and the encoding of the five frame-rate bytes. Answer: the spike in recommendation 1; or a newer Solomon datasheet revision.
- **Does 0x37 RAM ping-pong give a hardware old/new swap** that avoids re-uploading both planes? Answer: datasheet reading plus a bench test.
- **Which 0x22 value to use per scan.** The datasheet decoding points at 0xCC (clock and analog on, mode 2, no OTP load) or 0x0C once powered; a community SSD1677 note for the X4 uses 0x0C for its custom-LUT fast path and quotes timings without a source [31][36]. Answer: the spike.
- **Panel lifetime under one-frame, DC-unbalanced scans.** No vendor figure exists [15][16][19]. Answer: a long-run soak on a sacrificial unit, photographed daily.
- **Per-batch and per-unit variance across the three X4 Pro controllers** and the Sticky's panel, where the X4's 0x1C sequence already runs the full waveform [9]. Answer: the calibration tooling in recommendation 5 on several units.
- **Temperature.** InkBoy ignores the sensor; the OTP waveforms are temperature-indexed [1][17][18]. Answer: test at two temperatures during the spike.
- **Grayscale ground truth.** InkBoy's repository has no photo, video or reflectance data; the dose table was judged by eye [1]. Answer: a fixed camera over the transition-test screen, which also serves the lifetime soak.
- **Measured SPI and DMA cost on the ESP32-S3** for a 48 KB plane; none published [24]. Answer: part of the spike.
- **An SSD1677 equivalent of YACP's reinforcement row.** It would be a custom 0x32 LUT with non-zero LUT0 and LUT3 rows; no source has built one [31][43]. Answer: a second LUT in the spike, judged on the transition-test screen.
