## D6. YACP's no-flash screen maintenance on the X3

**Answer:** YACP's trick is a differential refresh whose LUT gives unchanged pixels a short, weak pulse of their own polarity, so every page turn re-settles the whole panel and the periodic black-and-white flash goes away. The waveform is the X3's own stock "AA pre-BW" transition, and the fork's SDK already carries that transition for the X3 and for both UltraChip X4 Pro batches; what YACP adds is the policy that uses it as maintenance. There is no SSD1677 equivalent in either project.

**What YACP is.** "Yet Another CrossPoint", a personal firmware for the X3 and X4 started from CrossInk 1.4.0, MIT, 74 stars and 54 commits as of 2026-09-25, focused on battery, wake time and rendering [43][44]. It vendors the SDK as a plain directory and auto-detects the X3's two controllers, UC8253 on original units and UC8279d on newer ones, with the UC8279d path still marked "PENDING HARDWARE VALIDATION" [43].

**The mechanism.** The reader's periodic refresh countdown, every 1 to 60 pages, calls the driver's grayscale-base entry with a fast fallback instead of a flashing half refresh; the README states "the reinforcement is the page turn itself, not a second display update" [43]. In the UC8253 driver that writes the new frame to the new-data RAM only, keeps the previous frame in the old-data RAM, sets the VCOM and data interval to 0xA9, loads a five-register bank recovered from the OEM firmware, and triggers one refresh. Changed pixels get the normal drive; unchanged white and black pixels get a gentle same-polarity drive instead of sitting idle, which is what the "reinforcement" is [43] (high confidence, code read). The bank differs from the normal fast bank by longer phase times and a second short group in which the white-to-white and black-to-black rows are no longer zero [43]. When the old-data RAM cannot be trusted (after grayscale, at boot, after a forced sync) it falls back to a normal refresh and then runs the same bank as a settle pass; images, popup residue, sleep transitions and manual full refreshes keep the flashing path, and the page after an image is forced to a half clean because a differential pass cannot clear gray residue [43]. The UC8279d driver does the same with its own command-prefixed bank, different interval values, and a forced temperature and cascade setting applied only for this pass [43].

**Other display-path changes.** A no-op fast settle of the same frame after every full sync because "first differential after a full garbles on X3"; boot full syncs cut from two to one, saving "a FAST refresh that costs 435 ms"; a split refresh trigger with an interrupt-backed completion wait; a second no-flash settle before sleep so white pixels do not drift gray in long standby [43]. Published numbers are wake-path only; there is no refresh duration or ghosting metric for the reinforcement pass, only a before-and-after video, and the release that shipped it says it "has not yet been installed and validated on X3 or X4 hardware" [43][44]. No third-party report on its effect was found [44].

**What the fork already has.** The same transition exists in this fork's SDK: the X3 driver carries the identical bank, and the UC8179 and UC8279 X4 Pro drivers carry `kGrayPreBwMid` and `kXtfPreBwMid`, described in the source as "the stock non-flashing previous->current transition waveform" recovered from the factory image, reachable through the panel interface's grayscale-base call, which "replaces the ordinary B/W activation and leaves analog power on" [9] (high confidence, lead read). It is used only as the pre-conditioning step before a grayscale page; the generic reader path does not call it. YACP's contribution is therefore the policy layer, about 50 lines of reader logic plus a setting, not a new waveform [43].

**What transfers to the game engine.** Three things. First, the pattern: a differential update whose hold rows are weakly active is the controller-side answer to the "whole panel slowly turns gray" problem that InkBoy solves with per-pixel budgets in software [1][43]; on the UltraChip batches the fork can try it tomorrow with the bank it already has. Second, the rule set around it: force a strong scrub after any grayscale content, settle once after every full refresh, settle before sleep [43]. Third, the instrumentation gap: like Ink-boy and InkBoy, YACP publishes no per-pass timing, so the fork's spike should log one. What does not transfer is any byte of the banks, which are X3 panel recoveries, and the whole idea on the SSD1677 batch, where the equivalent would have to be built as a custom 0x32 LUT with non-zero LUT0 and LUT3 rows, which no source has done [31][43].
