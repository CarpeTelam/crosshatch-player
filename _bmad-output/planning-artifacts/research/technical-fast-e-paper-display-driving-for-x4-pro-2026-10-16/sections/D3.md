## D3. Prior art and what the X4 Pro's controllers permit

**Answer:** every fast per-pixel e-paper precedent except InkBoy drives a bare panel; the X4 Pro cannot do that. But the SSD1677 datasheet shows the controller has the exact structure InkBoy exploits on the SSD1681, so the technique ports in principle. What no source gives is the frame period, which sets the floor.

**Bare-panel prior art, and why it does not transfer directly.** ZephRay's PaperBoy on the M5Stack PaperS3 reaches 30 to 60 fps by driving the 960x540 panel's raw row and column interface and skipping the waveform method entirely [14] (secondary reporting, medium confidence). His Glider README describes the fundamental per-pixel method: scan the TFT array at 50 to 120 Hz, push each changed pixel toward white or black, count frames and stop after a set period, and never drive pixels that did not change [12]. epdiy does the same with per-panel frame-time curves, a 4 to 5 frame binary mode, and temperature-dependent grayscale [13]. The same README rates integrated-controller panels at 100 to 300 ms per black and white partial update with total latency "from 100ms to several seconds" because of the host-to-controller data path [12]. The X4 Pro's panel has a controller on every batch, so this fork's only route is through the controller's LUT, as InkBoy did.

**What the SSD1677 exposes** (Solomon Systech datasheet Rev 1.0) [31]:

- A 112-byte waveform set: five LUTs of 10 groups with four 2-bit phases each (VSS, VSH1, VSL, VSH2), 10 groups of frame counts TP A to D and a repeat count RP, five frame-rate bytes, then gate voltage, VSH1, VSH2, VSL and VCOM. Command 0x32 writes the first 105 bytes; voltages go through 0x03, 0x04 and 0x2C (high confidence). This is byte-for-byte the layout the fork's SDK already uploads for its grayscale LUT, and at the SDK revision checked out here the voltage tail is the same five bytes Waveshare ships; upstream has since moved the VCOM byte after ghosting on the non-Pro X4 [9][32][8].
- Per-pixel LUT selection keyed on the pair of RAM bits (RED plane, BW plane): 00 to LUT0, 01 to LUT1, 10 to LUT2, 11 to LUT3, with LUT4 driving VCOM. The datasheet's "LUT2 = LUT0, LUT3 = LUT1" note for black and white panels is a waveform convention, not a hardware limit: Waveshare's 4-gray mode fills all four rows differently and activates them with 0x22 = 0xC7, which skips the OTP LUT load [31][32] (high confidence). That is InkBoy's selector (00 hold, 10 black, 01 white) in the SSD1677's own terms.
- Compared with InkBoy's SSD1681: the same 0x32 command and 2-bit voltage-select phases across the SSD168x family [26], with only layout differences: 10 phase groups instead of 12, five-byte timing rows, and 105 LUT bytes instead of 153 [35].
- Frame counts of 0 skip a phase and 1 is one frame; no minimum is stated, so a one-frame LUT is inside the documented ranges [31] (medium confidence on legality, by absence of a stated minimum).
- Command 0x22 is a stage mask. The datasheet lists 0xF7, 0xFF, 0xC7, 0xCF, 0x91, 0x99 and a few others by name (high confidence). Decoding the bits from those entries shows that bit 4 loads the OTP LUT and overwrites a 0x32 LUT. So 0xF7, 0xFF, 0xFC, 0xD7 and the fork's 0x1C DU path all run the OTP waveform, while 0xC7 and 0xCC run the loaded LUT, and 0xCC also keeps clock and analog alive between scans [31] (the decode of unlisted values such as 0x1C, 0xCC and 0x0C is derived, medium confidence). InkBoy counts the kept-alive analog as worth about 90 ms per update [1]. The fork's driver already uses 0xCC to activate its custom grayscale LUT [9].
- Command 0x37 selects "display mode 2" per waveform set and offers a "RAM ping-pong" for mode 2; the datasheet does not say how mode 2 combines the planes beyond the LUT-index table [31].
- The datasheet rates the SPI write interface at 20 MHz maximum [31]; the fork's SSD1677 driver defaults to 40 MHz [9], twice the rating.
- What the datasheet does not give: a Hz or ms value for the frame-rate bytes, a frame-rate table, or the gate-line-width commands 0x3A and 0x3B, which are reserved. The frame period at 800x480, and so the shortest one-frame update, is unevidenced [31]. The SSD1675 of the same family is adjustable from 15 to 200 Hz, 5 to 67 ms per frame, but that must not be assumed for the SSD1677 [33] (low confidence as a proxy).

**The UC8179 and UC8279 batches.** UltraChip parts keep the waveform in registers 0x20 to 0x24 as VCOM, WW, KW, WK and KK tables of 42 bytes, keyed on the old and new pixel values, with a panel-setting bit choosing register versus OTP LUTs [11][27]. They also have a PLL frame-rate register, 0x30, which the fork's UC8279 driver sets to 0x0E on the X4 Pro [8][9]. GxEPD2's partial LUT for the 800x480 GDEY075T7 already sets WW and KK to zero and drives only KW and WK, which is the same "hold unchanged pixels, drive changed ones" selector expressed as old/new pairs [11]. The fork's UC8179 driver uploads 42-byte register LUTs and uses TSSET as the frame-rate lever, and the upstream UC82xx drivers run differential DU with "promote to full every N refreshes" against ghosting [8][9]. So the per-pixel-budget emulation exists on all three batches, in two register dialects, each needing its own panel-tuned tables.
