## D4. Implementation reality on the ESP32-S3: floors, risk, and what teams report

**Answer:** the SPI floor is arithmetic only, the panel-side floor on controller parts is unmeasured outside InkBoy, panel damage from DC-unbalanced waveforms is documented but unquantified, and no team has shipped and reported on a custom fast mode over time.

**Throughput floor.** No measured SPI figure for a 48 KB e-paper frame on the ESP32-S3 was found; only arithmetic is available (derived). Wire time for one 800x480 1-bpp plane is 19.2 ms at 20 MHz and 9.6 ms at 40 MHz, doubled for both planes; an older ESP-IDF document limits the original ESP32's SPI to 40 MHz through the GPIO matrix, and no source found states the figure for the S3 [24] (medium). One full two-plane upload on the X4 Pro is therefore 19 to 38 ms of wire time before any waveform runs, which alone caps a whole-screen scan near 25 to 50 scans per second. Scaled from InkBoy, the per-pixel counter store grows from 25 KB to about 240 KB for dithered mode (four bit-planes plus target over 48 KB); D2a gives the larger grayscale figures [1][9] (derived).

**Panel-side floor on controller parts.** No SSD16xx or UC81xx project reporting a sustained update rate with a custom shortened LUT was found other than InkBoy itself [1]. The fastest documented hobbyist result besides PaperBoy is 20 fps 4-gray video on a bare 960x540 panel driven over its parallel bus from a stock ESP32-S3, calibrated by "lots of trial and error" against a flatbed scanner, with observers reporting heavy ghosting and grayscale remnants and blacks rendered as grays [19] (high confidence, 2026-07). Vendor and reference figures for the X4 Pro's exact panel class agree on the stock floor: GxEPD2 measured 1,567 ms full and 500 ms partial on the GDEQ0426T82 [10]; Waveshare quotes 4 s full and 0.7 s partial and comments its temperature-forced fast init at 1.5 s [32][34]; the current firmware measures 559 ms fast and 1.5 to 1.7 s full and half with the stock sequences, and about 77 to 85 ms with the opt-in 0x1C DU path [9]; reseller listings quote 0.42 to 1 s partial [25] (reseller figures low confidence, inconsistent among themselves). No published ghosting-versus-frame-count measurement exists for any of these controllers.

**The cheap trick everyone uses first.** On SSD16xx parts the common "fast" mode is not a custom LUT: GxEPD2's GDEQ0426T82 driver, the idfxx SSD1680 driver and Waveshare's 4.26" driver all force the temperature register (0x1A) before the update so the OTP picks a shorter, hotter waveform [10][18][32]. The X4 Pro driver already writes 0x5A there for its half refresh, and the UC8179 batch driver's comment calls its TSSET 0x5A "REQUIRED" for the shorter partial [9]. The idfxx author documents the cost: under-driven blacks that settle to visible gray on the next partial, quality that "varies by panel batch and temperature", and a full refresh every 10 or so partials to clear ghosting [18] (high confidence).

**Risk and durability: the evidence.** Two independent publisher classes say DC-unbalanced short waveforms can damage panels. A 2021 peer-reviewed waveform paper keeps DC balance "to prevent the charge from getting trapped in the driving backplane" and says fast waveforms that ignore it "may cause damage to the EPD" [15]; E Ink patents state that electrodes "may be irreversibly degraded" under extreme imbalance and that DC balance costs update times "two to three times longer" than imbalanced drive [16][17] (verified, two publishers). No quantified lifetime or refresh-count limit was found from E Ink or Solomon Systech; a Hackaday commenter's "~10M refreshes per pixel" is unverified [19].

**Risk and durability: the practice.** The only long-run user retrospective found is an Inky Frame refreshed every 10 minutes for months. It showed residue that grew over tens of thousands of refreshes and largely cleared with double refreshes, so the residue was not permanent; but that was a vendor OTP waveform, not a custom one [20] (medium). Vendor guidance converges on the same mitigation: a full clean every 5 to 10 partials (Inkplate) [21], conservative minimum intervals from Adafruit and Waveshare resellers [22], and InkBoy's hard caps [1].

**Calibration practice.** No open camera or photodiode rig was found; the documented methods are InkBoy's on-device transition test plus photos [1][5], Tony Weston's flatbed scanner [19], and epdiy's per-panel frame-time curves generated offline [13]. epdiy notes grayscale accuracy is temperature dependent (calibrated near 22 °C) and VCOM must be calibrated per module for partial updates [13]; the upstream SDK's own 2026-10-12 commit changed the SSD1677 custom-LUT VCOM byte after ghosting on the non-Pro X4 [8], which is the same lesson on this panel.

**Team-level reality.** No open e-reader firmware with a 6 to 12 month retrospective on a shipped custom fast mode was found. The nearest evidence is GxEPD2's author offering only a differential black/white refresh as an "experimental" fast path, with a user reporting "tens of milliseconds" updates on a UC8151D and "a few pixels don't get turned on all the way" [23]; Espressif's own SSD1681 component notes that powering the display on reloads the OTP LUT over a custom one [28]; and the open ESP-IDF driver ecosystem is thin, with one driver offering no LUT upload at all [28].
