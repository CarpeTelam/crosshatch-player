# Three first-party games ship with each release

## Description

Writes Sudoku, Ultimate tic-tac-toe, and Battleship as script packages that use only API level 1, which the fork release workflow of AD-25 packs and attaches to each release, and a PR-time check over `games/<id>/`. Their nearby rounds are epic-play-nearby's, and the API level 1 freeze and the starter repo epic-api-freeze's (owner, 2026-10-04).

## Outcome

Every release ships three games that a player installs and plays solo and in pass-and-play, hidden included; CAP-10's success check for those modes and CAP-1's "no game-specific firmware code" check are the signal (the nearby rounds close CAP-10 in epic-play-nearby).

## Requirements

This epic owns CAP-10 except the nearby rounds (epic-play-nearby; epic-script-runtime built the host API itself) and closes CAP-1's success check ("no game-specific firmware code"); epic-api-freeze closes CAP-1's versioned API by freezing level 1. Written at inception, 2026-10-04; lines without a CAP id cite their source.

- R1 (CAP-10; `first-party-games.md`; game-api-seed §1): three packages in `games/<id>/`, each with `api` 1: `sudoku` (`modes` `["solo"]`, `seats {1, 1}`, `hidden` false), `ultimate-tic-tac-toe` (`["pass"]`, `seats {2, 2}`, `hidden` false), and `battleship` (`["pass"]`, `seats {2, 2}`, `hidden` true). Manifests declare only `solo` or `pass`; epic-play-nearby adds `nearby` to the two-player games, so each two-player `draw` shows one seat's view and nothing but the manifest has to change. Each packs with `scripts/pack_game.py`. Fixtures and rounds stay under `test/game_script/fixtures/`, never in `games/`.
- R2 (CAP-1 success; SPEC Constraints): no game-specific C++: the epic changes nothing under `src/` or `lib/` and nothing in `docs/crosshatch/api-level-1.txt`, so it adds nothing to the API, needs no flash share, and leaves `API_SURFACE_CRC` alone; game rules live only in the Lua.
- R3 (CAP-10; `first-party-games.md`, Shared board): one 9×9 board module (the layout computed from `ch.screen.w` and `ch.screen.h`, about 50 px cells on the X4 Pro, a grid with heavier 3 × 3 block lines, cell rectangles, and the tap-to-cell mapping) is written once in `ultimate-tic-tac-toe` and copied byte for byte into `sudoku`, since `require` loads only from a package's own root; a test fails when the copies differ.
- R4 (CAP-10; `first-party-games.md`, Sudoku; owner Decisions 2026-10-04, after crossplay's Sudoku+ and Sudoku): Sudoku plays as Sudoku+ does: tap a cell, then a digit on a 3 × 3 pad (no text entry); tapping the selected cell deselects it, and a write lets go of the cell. NOTES toggles pencil mode; a mark is a dot in its digit's pad position (1 top-left, 9 bottom-right), a grey ring (a `"dark"` filled circle with a white one inside it) or, for the focused digit, solid black, and a menu toggle draws marks as small digits instead: 18 package images of about 10 × 14 px, nine with the 50% dither baked into their 1-bit pixels for the grey marks and nine solid black for the focused digit, drawn in the same 3 × 3 slots (`ch.gfx.image` is opaque, so each carries its own white paper over SHADE PEERS's `"light"` fill, which Sudoku+ paints by hand); `ch.gfx.text` cannot draw grey (Sudoku+ greys its numerals by redrawing odd anti-diagonals white, about 9,500 line calls for a full grid, over `frame_commands_count`) and would need Ubuntu 10's ascender hardcoded, since there is no `ch.text_height`; marks a peer's digit rules out are hidden at draw time, never erased. The focused digit's copies on the board are drawn emphasised. ERASE clears the selected cell's digit and notes; UNDO steps back one cell at a time, and one FILL NOTES as a single step, through a bounded ring kept in `state`. A digit that clashes with a peer is marked, and clues cannot be changed (`apply` rejects it with a reason). A menu panel, a full-page sheet drawn in place of the board, holds HINT (selects the cell the solver of R5 can prove next and names its rule, never the digit, and first selects a digit that disagrees with the answer), FILL NOTES (every legal candidate into every empty cell), CHECK (strikes the player's digits that disagree with the answer, or says all correct), and the toggles SHADE PEERS (the selected cell's row, column, and box drawn `"light"`), SHOW REMAINING (each pad key's count still to place), and notes as dots or digits; the toggles are remembered per device in `ch.store`. HINT's and CHECK's marks last until the next edit. No clock is drawn while solving: each move carries the time since the previous one and `apply` adds it to the elapsed time in `state`; a full grid with no clash ends the round with `winners = {1}`, the end screen shows the elapsed time, and on `over` `input` records the best time per difficulty in `ch.store`, except for a solve that used HINT.
- R5 (CAP-10; `first-party-games.md`, Sudoku; owner Decisions 2026-10-04): puzzles come from a bank in the package's `puzzles.lua`, 81 characters each, made and graded offline with crossplay's `SudokuCore` (the source commit and seeds recorded in the file) in its four bands, Easy (singles), Medium (locked candidates), Hard (pairs), and Expert (triples, X-wing, XY-wing, swordfish); difficulty is a manifest `settings` entry with those four values, read from `ctx.settings`; `setup` picks a puzzle of that difficulty at random and applies a random symmetry (digit relabelling, band, stack, row, and column swaps within their groups, transposition), so Play again deals a new grid, and `state` may hold the puzzle as its bank index and symmetry rather than its 81 characters. A Lua port of crossplay's technique ladder, the cheapest technique first, finds HINT's next step and solves the puzzle for HINT's and CHECK's answer within the 2 M-instruction budget on every bank puzzle. A host test checks every bank puzzle is a valid grid with exactly one solution, by a solution counter that shares no code with the solver, and that the solver finishes it. On-device generation is not in v1.
- R6 (CAP-10; `first-party-games.md`, Ultimate tic-tac-toe): standard rules: seat 1 plays x, seat 2 circle; the cell played sends the next player to the matching small board, and to any open small board when that one is won or full; three won small boards in a line win; a big board with no line left to make is a draw. The whole board is drawn with the module of R3, the small boards the player may play in are highlighted, the player taps a cell directly, and there is no zoomed view.
- R7 (CAP-10, CAP-6 as built; `first-party-games.md`, Battleship; owner Decisions 2026-10-04): a 10 × 10 board and a fleet of five ships (5, 4, 3, 3, 2). Placement runs one seat at a time (`status` names one turn seat): each ship placed is a move that keeps the turn, rotation is the seat's `ui`, a Random move places the remaining ships, and Ready, once all five are placed, passes the turn, so the runtime's Result and hand-off hide the fleet; then the seats alternate one shot per turn, a repeated shot is rejected with a reason, the mover sees hit, miss, or sunk in Result, and the first seat to sink the whole fleet wins. While firing, the target board is drawn large (about 44 px cells) and the seat's own fleet small (about 28 px cells) below it; each `draw` shows only its seat's fleet and its own shots, and seat 0 at Over shows both fleets.
- R8 (CAP-8 as built; Done when 3 as amended 2026-10-04): marks and controls come from `GameIcons`, such as `x` and `circle` (Ultimate tic-tac-toe), `fire`, `dot-outline` or `waves`, `boat`, `arrow-clockwise`, `shuffle`, and `check` (Battleship), and `pencil-simple`, `eraser`, `arrow-u-up-left`, `lightbulb`, and `check` (Sudoku); boards, Sudoku's digits and dot notes, and Battleship's small fleet board are drawn with `ch.gfx` shapes and text; every icon a game names is in `api-level-1.txt`'s `icon` entries. Sudoku ships its note digits as package images (R4); any game may also ship `icon.png`, `title.png`, or `handoff.png`.
- R9 (CAP-10; SPEC Constraints; `first-party-games.md`, Common rules; owner Decision 2026-10-04): every snapshot of every game, as the runtime encodes it, stays within 700 B, half of `state_bytes` (1,400), at every step of every round the games check plays; boards are packed strings (the inception shapes: Ultimate tic-tac-toe about 110 B, Sudoku about 550 to 620 B with the puzzle as bank index and symmetry, values, notes as a 9-bit mask per cell, the undo ring with one notes copy for FILL NOTES, the elapsed time, and a hint flag, Battleship about 260 B); a builder may choose any shape within the ceiling. Moves stay within `move_bytes`, and no call faults at the 2 M-instruction budget or the frame limits.
- R10 (CAP-10; Done when 5; owner Decision 2026-10-04): the games check is a harness target under `test/game_script/` that finds every `games/<id>/` at configure time (the root a CMake cache variable, so scratch trees stay in the build directory), packs each with `pack_game.py` at build time, installs it with the real installer, and plays every round under `test/game_script/fixtures/rounds/<id>/` in each declared mode, each round carrying its `settings` and a seed for the game's random source, drawing every local seat after each step; it fails on a pack or load failure, any fault, a snapshot over R9's ceiling, an outcome other than the round's file states, a game with no rounds, or rounds with no game. It runs in upstream's `unit-tests` job, which builds every target, and in the fork job `games-check` in `crosshatch-ci.yml`, which builds and runs only this target and is listed in `Crosshatch Test Status`'s `needs`, so the gate does not depend on upstream's workflow; the job's commands are run once from a fresh clone before the entry counts as built (AGENTS.md). It changes no upstream file (`test/CMakeLists.txt`, ledger row 3, already adds `game_script`). Epic-play-nearby's nearby changes must pass it.
- R11 (CAP-7, CAP-6 as built; `deferred-work.md` `## e5-close`, V3): each game survives sleep and Continue through the solo and pass resume epic-pass-and-play built; Battleship's placement keeps the turn on a move, so harness `PassResumeTest` cases resume a hidden pass match saved in Paused and one whose snapshot was written mid-turn in Playing, both during placement, into the hand-off with the placed ships kept.
- R12 (CAP-10 success, Done when 2; `deferred-work.md` `## e5-close` (5.13), `## e6pre-13`, `## e5-r5`, `## e5-r2`, `## owner-e4-games-cap`; epic-api-freeze Notes, the Lua C-stack item): on an X4 Pro the owner installs the three packages through the inbox and plays each a round in every mode it declares from Home in at most 3 taps, sleeps mid-round in each and continues (Battleship mid-placement too), checks 5.13's tap targets and a sleep from Over after a seat's move during Battleship, counts the `dropped a touch before the frame was on the panel` lines after "I'm ready", runs B7.6 with the `pass-store.chgame` that `scripts/pack_device_run.py` writes into the packet, and records each game's "VM stopped" line (arena peak and stack high-water) and the launcher's free heap with the three games installed; `owner-e4-games-cap`'s investigation stays deferred (its trigger is a games epic after epic-play-nearby, and three games are far from the 64-game cap), with the heap figure added to its entry.
- R13 (CAP-10; Done when 4; spine AD-25): a dry run of `crosshatch-release.yml` on the epic head packs the three games; after the epic PR merges, a publishing release from `develop` attaches `sudoku.chgame`, `ultimate-tic-tac-toe.chgame`, and `battleship.chgame`, each with the hash `pack_game.py` prints for `games/<id>/` at the released commit.
- R14 (Done when 6; AGENTS.md; `docs/crosshatch/orchestrated-epics.md`): the epic merges to `develop` as one PR with the five-env build, both test-status jobs, the whole-tree format check, `pio check`, and the fork-only size and ledger jobs green; the open items the epic meets are fixed, documented, or deferred in the refactor sweep, each deferral with a reason the owner confirms at the device run.

## Done when

1. Sudoku (solo), Ultimate tic-tac-toe (pass), and Battleship (pass, hidden) are script packages in `games/<id>/`, and no game-specific C++ exists in the firmware; their manifests declare only `solo` or `pass`.
2. Each packs with `pack_game.py`, installs through the inbox, and plays a round on a device in every mode it declares.
3. All three stay within API level 1 and the 1,400 B snapshot, add nothing to the API, and draw their marks and controls from `GameIcons`; boards, Sudoku's digits and notes, and Battleship's small fleet board use `ch.gfx` shapes and text (amended at inception, 2026-10-04).
4. A fork release made by the release workflow (AD-25) has the three `.chgame` files attached, packed byte-for-byte from `games/<id>/`.
5. A PR that breaks a game in `games/<id>/` (it fails to pack, to load, or to play a scripted round within the snapshot limit and the instruction budget) fails a check before merge.
6. Merged to `develop` with the five-env build, host suites, whole-tree format check, `pio check`, and the fork-only size and ledger jobs all green.
